## What to expect

NIS2 entered into force in January 2023 with Member State transposition due by October 2024. Most ANSPs are classified as "essential entities" under the directive. This session covers:

- **ANSP classification** under NIS2 — essential vs important, and what the difference means
- **Incident reporting** — the 24-hour initial notification and 72-hour detailed report requirements
- **Supply chain security** — NIS2 Article 21 obligations for ANSP vendor management
- **Board liability** — the personal accountability provisions and what they mean for ANSP leadership
- **ENISA guidance** for aviation sector NIS2 implementation

## Key questions this session will address

**Are ANSPs classified as essential or important entities under NIS2?**
ANSPs providing ATC services are classified as essential entities. This triggers the full NIS2 obligation set including 24-hour incident reporting, comprehensive security measures, and board-level accountability. Ground handling and airport operators may be classified as important entities with lighter requirements.

**What must ANSPs report and when?**
NIS2 requires an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, an incident notification within 72 hours, and a final report within one month. "Significant" is defined by impact on service continuity, not by technical severity.

**What are board members personally liable for under NIS2?**
NIS2 Article 20 requires that management bodies of essential entities approve and oversee cybersecurity risk management measures. Members who fail to comply can face personal liability. This is a significant governance change for ANSP boards.
